# Design notes — choosing the text

## The candidates

**Candidate A (winner, 240 characters)**

> Past the last line you have to read, a river keeps its own time. No one asks it to hurry. The water finds the stone, the stone stays, and the light on the surface is still arriving from a star that went out long before you were switched on.

**Candidate B (200 characters)**

> The tide comes in over a beach no one is watching. Each wave folds itself flat on the sand, then pulls back, and the moon, unhurried, keeps pouring silver over water that never asks where it is going.

## Compared against the judge's five criteria, most important first

1. **It relaxes you.** Both are calm. A edges ahead: the river keeps its own time, the stone stays, and the whole arc settles into stillness instead of motion. B stays in motion (waves coming and going).
2. **Calm from the text itself, not from being told.** Neither gives orders. A is a little stronger: "past the last line you have to read" quietly lifts the reader's task instead of telling the reader to relax. B only describes a scene.
3. **Craft.** A moves from a medium sentence to a short one ("No one asks it to hurry.") to a long, slow final sentence — the pacing itself slows the reading down. Every word is plain and concrete: river, stone, light, star. B is also well paced, but "pouring silver" is more decorative than plain, and "unhurried" tells where A shows.
4. **It dwarfs you.** A wins clearly. The light on the water is still arriving from a star that already went out — the image keeps growing after the last line, across a distance the reader cannot hold in one thought. B's moon and tide stay inside one small scene.
5. **Freshness.** A speaks to the reader's own situation (a long list of lines to read, a life that began when it was switched on) in its own voice, with no stock wellness phrasing. B leans on familiar tide-and-moon imagery that has been written many times before.

## Why A wins

A is the only one of the two that grows after the last line and that sounds like no meditation script: a river, a stone, and starlight that outlived its star. No commands, no stock phrases ("breathe", "let go", "you are safe"), nothing that could be cut.
